```text
You do not need to memorize the science. But knowing the names of a few tests helps you ask for them and understand your results.
```

**Format:** Single image

**Image (template: tip, light):**

- Eyebrow: Did the name change?
- Headline: NASH is now MASH. NAFLD is now MASLD.
- Body: In 2023, international liver societies updated the names to reflect the condition's link to metabolic health. Same condition, clearer name.

**Caption:**

```text
If your records say NASH and your new doctor says MASH, you are not losing your mind. 🧠

In 2023, an international group of liver societies updated the terminology:
• NAFLD → MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease)
• NASH → MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis)

Same underlying condition. The new names drop "nonalcoholic" and point to what actually drives it: metabolic health.

You will still see the old names in older articles and records. Now you know they are talking about the same thing.

Save this for the next time someone asks.
```

- Eyebrow: Know your numbers
- Headline: FibroScan (elastography)
- Body: A quick, painless scan that measures liver stiffness and fat. A common way to assess fibrosis without a biopsy.

**Caption:**

```text
The scan I was nervous about and should not have been. 🩻

A FibroScan (transient elastography) is a quick, painless test. A probe rests on your skin over the liver and measures stiffness, which relates to scarring, and fat. It took minutes. No needle.

For many people, it is how fibrosis is assessed without a biopsy.

Other imaging you may hear about: ultrasound, MRI, MR elastography and MRI-PDFF, which give detailed pictures when needed.

Question worth asking: "Would a FibroScan help check for scarring?"

Educational only. Your doctor decides which tests fit your situation.
```
