```text
The most hopeful fact in all of medicine, in one number. 💚
```

**Caption:**

```text
Friday question, with a number attached. 📝

"Can you calculate my FIB-4 from my last blood test?"

FIB-4 uses four things you probably already have on file: your age, ALT, AST and platelet count. No new needle. Here is roughly how the result is read, according to US liver society guidance:

• Below 1.3: lower likelihood of advanced scarring.
• Between 1.3 and 2.67: unclear, so a FibroScan or a blood fibrosis test usually comes next.
• Above 2.67: higher likelihood, and a liver specialist is worth asking about.

The bands shift a little with age and your doctor interprets them in context. The point is that the answer may already be sitting in your chart.
```

**Caption:**

```text
Friday question, week six. 📝

"What is my liver stiffness number, and what does it mean?"

If you have had a FibroScan, you were given a number in kilopascals, kPa. It measures how stiff the liver is, and stiffness tracks with scarring. US liver society guidance reads it roughly like this:

• Under 8: advanced scarring is unlikely.
• 8 to 12: unclear, so follow-up or another test.
• Over 12: advanced scarring is more likely, and specialist care matters.

Ask for the number itself, not just "it looks okay." Write it down. Next year's number only means something next to this one.
```

**Format:** Single image (editorial card)

**Image (template: editorial, white):**

- Headline: Sugary drinks go *straight to the liver*.
- Body: In a 2021 trial, two large sodas' worth of added sugar a day *roughly doubled* the liver's own fat production within weeks. Weight barely moved.
- Footnote: Source: Geidl-Flueck et al., Journal of Hepatology 2021

**Caption:**

```text
The ingredient my liver responds to fastest. 🥤

In a 2021 study, healthy young men drank beverages adding 80 grams of fructose or table sugar a day for seven weeks. That is about two large sodas. Their livers roughly doubled their own fat production, measured directly. Weight barely changed.

The liver handles fructose almost alone, and when there is more than it needs, it turns the surplus into fat and stores it right there.

I did not quit sugar. I quit drinking it. Sparkling water with lemon, unsweetened iced tea, and coffee without the syrup. That one swap moved my numbers more than any other.
```
